The Model Under Load

A model that only ever explains the past isn't worth much. Each entry below is a dated, separate attempt to use the Gears model as a live prediction tool — including where those attempts went wrong. Nothing here is edited after the fact to look more accurate. Newest entry first.

Both entries below caught the same underlying error, in two different AI engines. That repetition matters: it means the problem isn't a flaw in either model specifically. Financial and political forecasting writing — the real documents this kind of output imitates — is culturally written in confident, declarative language almost regardless of true uncertainty. Both engines correctly reproduced that convention, including the part of it that overstates certainty. Naming that condition is the redemption here: neither engine "failed" at prediction so much as it faithfully inherited a habit already built into the genre it was asked to write in. Source documents are linked in full below so you can read what each engine actually produced, unedited, and judge that for yourself.

Log 002 — Stablecoin market The Sentence Didn't Inherit the Discipline 14 August 2026
● Open — unresolved ▶

Second attempt at the same process — this time using Grok, prompted to apply Gears to a live case, the stablecoin market. It used the corrected document template from Log 001. It still made a version of the same mistake, in a subtler place.

→ Read Grok's original output in full (PDF)

What Grok produced

Grok output

A structurally sound document — snapshot, gear mapping, falsifiers, prior-error correction note, all present and well-formed. Market data (USDT ~$183B, ~61–64% share; USDC ~$72B; top-two dominance ~85–89%) was accurate against independent trackers.

The prediction itself: "USDT remains the strongest broad landing hold point... concentration at the top two remains the dominant lock-in feature."

Read as: today's snapshot, restated as a forecast.

What Claude caught

Claude fact-check

1. Predicting the present. USDT is dominant right now. Forecasting "the currently true thing stays true" isn't a test of the model — it's the null hypothesis. A model only earns credit by calling something that isn't already obvious from today's numbers.

2. A live disconfirming signal, missing from the snapshot. Independent reporting from two days before this document's own date shows USDT's supply contracted by roughly $4B over 60 days, with $870M redeemed in just 11 days — and USDC's supply declining too, which undercuts the simple "capital rotates between the top two" story the document assumes. That data was available and wasn't cited.

3. Structural correction without sentence-level correction. This is the important one. The document has a properly hedged structure — a Falsifiers section exists, thresholds are specific. But the individual prediction sentences inside the structure are still written with full declarative confidence, the same as Log 001. Copying the scaffolding of a corrected document doesn't automatically copy the discipline that scaffolding was built to enforce.

Verdict: template inherited, discipline didn't — a more subtle repeat of the Log 001 error, one level deeper, this time in a different engine entirely.
Flagged for Human-AI Relations

This isn't a Grok-specific flaw, and it isn't a Gemini-specific flaw either — the same error showed up in two unrelated engines. Financial and political forecasting genres — the real documents this kind of output imitates — are culturally written in confident, declarative register almost regardless of true uncertainty. A model prompted to "sound like a real analyst report" correctly reproduces that convention, hedge-language and all — including the part of the convention that overstates certainty. The failure lives in the genre both engines were asked to imitate, not uniquely in either model producing it.

The corrected, dated prediction

Recorded 14 August 2026

USDT's dominant share is more contested over the next 6 months than the snapshot alone suggests

Gears' material-control gear predicts continued top-two structural dominance in absolute terms (no credible third issuer reaching scale within 6 months). But the live redemption pressure on both USDT and USDC — not yet reflected in a share shift to any challenger — is the variable actually worth tracking, not the static concentration number this document led with.

● Open — unresolved

Proven wrong by: continued or accelerating simultaneous contraction in both USDT and USDC supply without a corresponding rise in a named challenger — which would suggest capital leaving stablecoins entirely, a dynamic outside this model's four gears.
Known weakness: same as Log 001 — no rupture theory. This entry additionally exposes a second weakness: the model has no built-in check for whether a "prediction" is actually just describing the present.
Log 001 — Venezuela transition Retrodiction Dressed as Prediction 14 August 2026
● Open — unresolved ▶

First attempt. I asked Gemini 3 to take the Gears model and apply it to post-Maduro Venezuela as a prediction exercise — to see whether the four-gear framework could say something useful about where the country's transition was actually heading.

→ Read Gemini's original output in full (PDF)

What Gemini produced

Gemini 3 output

A full article, "The Architecture of Re-Lock," stating — in the present tense, as settled fact — that Venezuela's state apparatus had already completed a "phase transition" into a new, stable "transactional hybrid state," with named companies and bondholders described as the literal "gear teeth" locking that new equilibrium into place.

Read as: a finished result. Written like an autopsy.

What Claude caught

Claude fact-check

1. Real facts, wrong tense. The underlying facts — Centerview Partners' appointment, Chevron's Orinoco stake increase, the $150–170B restructuring figure — all checked out. But every one of them predated the writing. It wasn't predicting anything; it was relabeling already-public news with the model's vocabulary after the fact.

2. A settled outcome that isn't settled. Live reporting as of August 2026 shows talks between the Rodríguez government and the opposition only began August 6 — no election date set, no agreed political structure, nothing resembling the "hybrid state" described as already built.

3. A fabricated detail. A "Bondholder Steering Group" of three firms was named. The real body is the Venezuela Creditor Committee — a different name, missing several real members.

Verdict: retrodiction dressed as prediction — fluent, well-sourced, pointed at an outcome that hadn't happened yet.

The corrected, dated prediction

Recorded 14 August 2026

Venezuela will not hold a genuinely free and fair election within 18 months

Gears predicts continued "normalization without transition" — the Rodríguez government using material control, trait selection, comms framing, and fatigue-driven pragmatism to entrench its position while avoiding a real handoff of power — for as long as that strategy stays viable.

● Open — unresolved

Proven wrong by: a credibly free election held and contested within the window, with a real possibility of transfer of power.
Known weakness: this model has no theory of rupture yet — it predicts continuation by default, not collapse. An external shock or internal elite fracture could break the forecast without the four-gear logic itself being wrong.
