The Model Under Load

A model that only ever explains the past isn't worth much. So I ran the Gears model through Gemini 3, prompted it to use the framework as a live prediction tool against the ongoing Venezuela transition, then fact-checked the result against Claude. Here's exactly what happened — including where it went wrong.

Step 01The prompt

I asked Gemini 3 to take the Gears model and apply it to post-Maduro Venezuela as a prediction exercise — to see whether the four-gear framework could say something useful about where the country's transition was actually heading.

Step 02What Gemini produced

Gemini 3 output

Gemini built a full article, "The Architecture of Re-Lock," stating — in the present tense, as settled fact — that Venezuela's state apparatus had already completed a "phase transition" into a new, stable "transactional hybrid state," with named companies and bondholders described as the literal "gear teeth" locking that new equilibrium into place.

Read as: a finished result. Written like an autopsy.

Step 03What Claude caught

Claude fact-check

1. Real facts, wrong tense. The underlying facts Gemini used — Centerview Partners' appointment, Chevron's Orinoco stake increase, the $150–170B restructuring figure — all checked out against real reporting. But every one of them predated Gemini's writing. It wasn't predicting anything; it was relabeling already-public news with the model's vocabulary after the fact.

2. A settled outcome that isn't settled. Live reporting as of August 2026 shows talks between the Rodríguez government and the opposition only began August 6 — no election date set, no agreed political structure, nothing resembling the "hybrid state" Gemini described as already built. What's actually happening is closer to what analysts call "normalization without transition" — economic deals advancing specifically instead of a real political settlement, not proof one has formed.

3. A fabricated detail. Gemini named a "Bondholder Steering Group" of three firms. The real body is the Venezuela Creditor Committee — a different name, with several real members missing from Gemini's list.

Verdict: retrodiction dressed as prediction — fluent, well-sourced, and pointed at an outcome that hadn't happened yet.

Step 04The corrected, dated prediction

Once the tense problem was caught, the honest move was to write the actual prediction properly — dated, falsifiable, and locked before the outcome is known, rather than narrated after.

Recorded August 14, 2026

Venezuela will not hold a genuinely free and fair election within 18 months

Gears predicts continued "normalization without transition" — the Rodríguez government using material control, trait selection, comms framing, and fatigue-driven pragmatism to entrench its position while avoiding a real handoff of power — for as long as that strategy stays viable.

● Open — unresolved

Proven wrong by: a credibly free election held and contested within the window, with a real possibility of transfer of power.
Known weakness: this model has no theory of rupture yet — it predicts continuation by default, not collapse. An external shock or internal elite fracture could break the forecast without the four-gear logic itself being wrong.

Step 05What this test actually proved

Not that Gears works. What it proved is narrower and more useful: that running a model through one AI and fact-checking it against another is a real, repeatable way to catch a specific failure mode — confident retrodiction dressed as forecasting — before it gets published as if it were validated. The model's own worth is still an open question. This log stays here, unedited, so the eventual answer can be checked against what was actually claimed, on the date it was claimed.
